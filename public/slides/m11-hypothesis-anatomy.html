Anatomy of a hypothesis · 4 blocks each
✗Bad hypothesis — guesswork
«Let's make the button green — maybe conversion will go up.»
1change
button → green
2outcome
— no metric named
3reasoning
— why green?
4evidence
— no data, just a hunch
1 of 4 blocks filled · "maybe" is not a metric · cannot be tested
✓Good hypothesis — testable
«We believe that changing the CTA from grey to green will lift CTR by 15%, because green reads as action and the current grey CTA blends into the background — heatmap shows 70% of users miss the CTA.»
1change
CTA · grey → green
2outcome
CTR ↑ by 15%
3reasoning
green = action; grey blends in
4evidence
heatmap: 70% miss the CTA
4 of 4 blocks filled · concrete metric · can be A/B tested
What each block answers
  1. 1ChangeWhat exactly are you altering? One thing, named precisely.
  2. 2OutcomeWhich metric moves, by how much, in which direction.
  3. 3ReasoningThe mental model that connects change → outcome.
  4. 4EvidenceWhat you already know (heatmap, interview, prior test) that justifies the bet.
If you can't fill all four — you're not ready to test. Go back and find the missing block first.
